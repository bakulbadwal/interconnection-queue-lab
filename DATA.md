**`data/queue.json` is an adaptation of that file, redistributed under CC BY 4.0 with attribution to LBNL and GridTracker.** The changes:

1. Dropped rows with status `unknown` (10) and rows with no request date (664).
2. Kept these fields only: region, a collapsed technology class, total MW, request date, status, event date. **Developer, project, utility and point-of-interconnection names are not included.**
3. **Capacity** = `mw_1 + mw_2 + mw_3`, with negatives treated as 0 (hybrids count every component). LBNL's published file excludes its own imputed storage MW for hybrids, so neither does this.
4. **Technology** collapsed from `type_clean` into 8 classes: Solar, Solar+Storage (`Solar+Battery`), Wind, Offshore wind, Battery, Gas (`Gas`, `Gas+Oil`), Nuclear, Other (everything else, including other hybrids).
5. **Status** collapsed to three: in the queue (`active` and `suspended`), operational, withdrawn.
6. **Event date** = `on_date` for operational projects, `wd_date` for withdrawn ones. Dates are stored as days since 2000-01-01.
7. **Timing sample flag** (`tm`): requests in CAISO, MISO, PJM or SPP that entered 2010–2025. Within that set, at least 90% of operational and of withdrawn rows carry their event date (checked 2026-10-22; ERCOT was 60% for withdrawals, ISO-NE 0% for operation dates, NYISO, Southeast and West under 65%). Rows in those cells whose outcome has no date (176) are left out of the timing sample. Rows whose event precedes the request (54, all regions) are excluded from timing everywhere. Events dated after 2025-12-31 (52, all regions) count as still in the queue in the timing sample.
8. **Censoring:** requests still in the queue are censored at 2025-12-31. Requests whose COD or withdrawal is dated after 2025-12-31 (52) count as still in the queue at the end of 2025, everywhere in the lab.
9. **Follow-up:** every odds readout stops at the last time at least 10 requests in the slice are still being watched. Beyond that, the odds are unknown, not zero.

Row counts at every step are in [`data/SNAPSHOT.md`](data/SNAPSHOT.md), regenerated by `make data`.

### Validation against LBNL's own numbers

`make check` reproduces LBNL's 2026 headline from this snapshot: of capacity that entered 2000–2020, **13%** reached commercial operation and **75%** was withdrawn (by project count: **19%** operational).

LBNL's report gives a **60.8-month** median request→COD for projects online in 2025 (sheet `37. IR to COD - all`, n = 294). Counting every row with a 2025 online date, whatever its status, `fetch_clean.py` gets 60.83 months over 296 rows. That set includes 133 rows not marked operational, 96 of them SPP requests marked *withdrawn*. The remaining gap of two rows (296 vs 294) is unexplained. The lab's own convention counts **operational projects only**, which gives 65 months over 163 projects across all nine regions. Step 2 shows both.

### Known limits

- **Generators, not loads.** A data center's own large-load request goes through a separate process that this file doesn't cover.
- **Timing comes from four regions.** ERCOT, the busiest data-center market, can't be timed from this file.
- **Dropped undated rows** could bias the timing sample if missing dates correlate with duration. It's 176 of 15,149 rows in the timing cells.
- **Not every uprate is identified.** LBNL's codebook notes that not all upgrades or uprates are flagged as such.
- The file is a snapshot. Its year-over-year sample can change as missing records are filled in (LBNL's note).

## 3. Teaching values (labelled on the page)

All in [`data/options.json`](data/options.json), shared by the page and the Python twins.

| Value | Default | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Value of a megawatt-year | $3.48M | Compute Deal Desk step 1's shadow price of the grid wall, itself at teaching prices (per-deal compute pricing isn't public) |
| Discount rate | 10% | A round cost of capital |
| Campus ready after filing | 3 years | Round figure for building and fitting out a campus |
| Brief (capstone) | ≥200 of 250 MW by end-2029 | A plausible deal requirement |
| Fallback year for a failed path | 8 years | Where failed MW come from eventually |
| Odds and most-likely times for the expedited track, uprate, restart, behind-the-meter gas and flexible load | see `options.json` | These paths have no public track record to measure. Their low and high times are anchored on the cited dates where one exists. |

The queue path in steps 3–4 is **measured**, not modeled. Its times are the request→COD waits of the 48 PJM gas plants of 100 MW or more that entered 2010–2020 and reached COD. Its odds are the Aalen–Johansen probability of COD across all 238 such requests, read at 8.4 years: the end of their follow-up (the last point with at least 10 requests still being watched), not 10 years.

## 4. What wasn't used, and why

- **interconnection.fyi / GridTracker web data:** its footer reads "All rights reserved" and no reuse license is stated. The lab uses the LBNL file, which GridTracker co-licenses under CC BY 4.0.
- **GridStatus app:** a paid product. The open-source `gridstatus` library could rebuild live ISO queues, but those don't carry the cleaned, cross-ISO outcome history this lab needs.
- **No course or case material** of any kind.
